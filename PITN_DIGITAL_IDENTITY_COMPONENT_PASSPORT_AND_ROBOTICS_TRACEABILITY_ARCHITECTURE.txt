DOCUMENT STATUS
Declarative Framework Architecture
Part of the POWER IN THE NUMBERS (PITN) Framework

This document defines the relationship between persistent digital identity, physical component identity, product identity, robot identity, digital passports, bills of materials, lifecycle records, and evidence within the PITN framework architecture.

It establishes the architectural relationship between PITN identity concepts and the ROBOPARTS.ai robotics component information model.

This document describes framework architecture and data relationships. It is not an implementation guide, development roadmap, commercial proposal, or claim that every described service is deployed as production software.

1. ARCHITECTURAL PURPOSE

PITN establishes a persistent identity architecture for physical assets, products, components, and their associated information.

The architecture connects an identifiable physical object to a structured digital record containing its identity, product classification, manufacturer association, configuration, installation, lifecycle history, and supporting evidence.

Within robotics, this architecture provides a common identity and information structure connecting:

- Robot assets
- Robot product models
- Robot configurations
- Component products
- Individual physical components
- Manufacturers and suppliers
- Engineering bills of materials
- Physical installation records
- Maintenance and lifecycle events
- Verification records and supporting evidence
- Digital component and robot passports

ROBOPARTS.ai represents the robotics component and lifecycle information architecture within this framework.

PITN provides the overarching identity and information architecture connecting these records.

2. FUNDAMENTAL IDENTITY PRINCIPLES

PITN distinguishes three fundamental identity classes.

2.1 Robot Identity

A robot identity identifies an individual robot asset.

Example:

ROBOT-001

This identifier represents one identifiable robot asset. It does not represent every robot of the same model or manufacturer.

2.2 Component Product Identity

A component product identity identifies a defined product or product model.

Example:

ACT-220

This identifier represents an actuator product model. It identifies the product definition rather than an individual physical actuator.

2.3 Physical Component Identity

A physical component identity identifies one individual manufactured component.

Example:

RP-CI-000001

Manufacturer serial number:

SN-1001

The persistent component-instance identity and manufacturer serial number are separate identifiers associated with the same physical component.

The internal component-instance identity provides a stable reference within the ROBOPARTS.ai information architecture.

The manufacturer serial number provides the manufacturer's physical product identification reference.

2.4 Identity Separation

The identity architecture preserves the distinction between:

ROBOT-001
Individual robot asset

ACT-220
Actuator product model

RP-CI-000001
Individual physical component record

SN-1001
Manufacturer serial number

These identifiers are related through structured records. They are not interchangeable.

3. PITN IDENTITY AND NAMESPACE ARCHITECTURE

PITN supports namespace-based classification and identity relationships.

The namespace:

.actuator

identifies the actuator component category within the identity architecture.

A namespace establishes a classification context. It does not replace the persistent identity of a product or physical component.

An actuator product may therefore be represented through:

Namespace: .actuator
Product identity: ACT-220
Manufacturer: Example Motion Systems
Manufacturer part number: EMS-ACT-220
Product revision: A

An individual physical actuator may be represented through:

Namespace: .actuator
Product identity: ACT-220
Component instance: RP-CI-000001
Manufacturer serial: SN-1001

The namespace, product identity, component-instance identity, and manufacturer serial number retain their individual meanings.

4. ROBOPARTS.ai IDENTITY AND COMPONENT MODEL

The ROBOPARTS.ai data architecture defines relationships among the following principal entities:

ORGANIZATION
Manufacturer, supplier, or other participating organization.

PRODUCT
A defined component product or product model.

COMPONENTINSTANCE
An individual physical component associated with a product.

ROBOT
An identifiable robot asset.

BOMPOSITION
The physical installation relationship between a component instance and a robot.

LIFECYCLEEVENT
A recorded event associated with a component's lifecycle.

COMPATIBILITYASSESSMENT
A structured compatibility relationship or assessment.

PERFORMANCEOBSERVATION
A recorded performance observation associated with an identifiable entity.

ONTOLOGYRELATIONSHIP
A structured relationship between identities or entities.

These entities form a connected information architecture rather than independent product records.

4.1 Entity Identity Relationships

The principal identity relationships are:

ROBOT
  robot_id: ROBOT-001

PRODUCT
  product_id: ACT-220
  namespace: .actuator

COMPONENTINSTANCE
  component_instance_id: RP-CI-000001
  product_id: ACT-220
  serialnumber: SN-1001

BOMPOSITION
  robotid: ROBOT-001
  componentinstanceid: RP-CI-000001

The robot identifies the asset.

The product identifies the component model.

The component instance identifies the individual physical component.

The BOM position records the installation relationship between the robot and that physical component.

4.2 Manufacturer Identity

Manufacturer information is associated with the product and its corresponding organization record.

Relevant product attributes include:

- Manufacturer
- Manufacturer part number
- Product namespace
- Product revision
- Product description
- Product specifications
- Product documentation
- Verification status
- Supporting evidence references

Manufacturer identity is not substituted for component identity.

4.3 Persistent Component Identity

A physical component retains its component-instance identity throughout its recorded lifecycle.

Its installation, removal, maintenance, inspection, replacement, and other lifecycle events are associated with that persistent identity.

A change in installation location does not create a new physical component identity.

A replacement component receives its own component-instance identity and manufacturer serial number.

A robot passport presents the structured identity and associated information of an individual robot asset.

The passport connects the robot identity to its product model, manufacturer, configuration, component relationships, installation records, lifecycle history, and supporting evidence.

The robot identity record may contain:

The identity record provides a common logical structure for associating identity information with different entity types.

The identity record is an architectural abstraction. It does not imply that every listed field is already implemented as a separate database table.

7. BILL OF MATERIALS ARCHITECTURE

PITN and ROBOPARTS.ai distinguish product configuration from physical component installation.

These represent two related but different information relationships.

7.1 Engineering Configuration BOM

The engineering configuration BOM represents the intended component composition of a robot model or defined configuration.

It identifies required products before individual physical components have necessarily been manufactured, procured, or installed.

The configuration BOM includes:

- Robot model
- Configuration identity
- Configuration revision
- Component product identity
- Required quantity
- Component specifications
- Approved alternatives
- Engineering approval references

The configuration BOM describes what a robot configuration is designed to contain.

7.2 Physical Installation BOM

The physical installation BOM represents the actual component instances installed in an individual robot asset.

The ROBOPARTS.ai BOMPosition entity represents this physical installation relationship.

Its documented fields include:

- robotid
- componentinstanceid
- position
- configurationrevision
- installedat
- removedat

The physical installation BOM describes what is installed in a particular robot, where it is installed, and when the installation relationship exists.

7.3 Relationship Between the Two BOM Structures

The engineering configuration BOM identifies the intended product composition.

The physical installation BOM identifies the actual physical component instances.

For example:

ENGINEERING CONFIGURATION

Robot model: HX-1
Configuration: CONFIG-01
Required product: ACT-220
Position: LEFT_SHOULDER

PHYSICAL ROBOT ASSET

Robot: ROBOT-001
Installed product: ACT-220
Component instance: RP-CI-000001
Manufacturer serial: SN-1001
Position: LEFT_SHOULDER

The relationship permits comparison between the intended configuration and the actual installed components.

The engineering BOM and physical installation BOM are distinct architectural concepts. The existing BOMPosition entity represents physical installation; a separate model-level BOM and BOMLine structure represents the engineering configuration relationship.

8. LIFECYCLE AND TRACEABILITY ARCHITECTURE

A physical component's identity is associated with its lifecycle history.

Lifecycle records reference the persistent component-instance identity rather than relying exclusively on a product name or manufacturer serial number.

Lifecycle events may include:

- Manufacture
- Registration
- Receiving
- Inspection
- Verification
- Installation
- Commissioning
- Operation
- Maintenance
- Repair
- Removal
- Replacement
- Retirement

A lifecycle event may contain:

- Event identity
- Component-instance identity
- Event type
- Event timestamp
- Responsible actor or organization
- Event payload
- Evidence references
- Related robot identity
- Related installation position

Example:

{
  "component_instance_id": "RP-CI-000001",
  "event_type": "COMPONENT_INSTALLED",
  "actor": "AUTHORIZED_TECHNICIAN",
  "payload": {
    "robot_id": "ROBOT-001",
    "position": "LEFT_SHOULDER",
    "configuration_revision": "CONFIG-01"
  },
  "evidence_references": [
    "INSTALLATION-RECORD-001"
  ]
}

The lifecycle record connects the individual component to the event and its supporting evidence.

9. PHYSICAL IDENTIFICATION AND DIGITAL RESOLUTION

Physical identification connects an identifiable physical component to its digital identity record.

Identification methods may include:

- QR codes
- Manufacturer serial numbers
- RFID
- NFC
- Other machine-readable identifiers

A physical identifier provides a reference through which the corresponding digital identity record can be resolved.

The architectural relationship is:

PHYSICAL COMPONENT
        |
        v
PHYSICAL IDENTIFIER
        |
        v
IDENTITY RESOLUTION
        |
        v
COMPONENTINSTANCE
        |
        v
PRODUCT IDENTITY
        |
        v
ROBOT INSTALLATION
        |
        v
BOM AND LIFECYCLE RECORDS
        |
        v
COMPONENT PASSPORT

An illustrative identity address is:

https://www.roboparts.ai/identity/SN-1001

This address illustrates the intended resolution relationship. It is not a declaration that this exact public route is operational.

A QR code or serial number identifies or locates a record. It does not, by itself, prove that the physical component is authentic.

The architecture distinguishes:

IDENTITY RESOLUTION
Determining which digital record corresponds to an identifier.

REGISTRATION
Establishing an identity record within the information system.

MANUFACTURER ASSOCIATION
Associating the component with a manufacturer and product record.

PHYSICAL IDENTIFICATION
Associating a machine-readable or human-readable identifier with the physical object.

AUTHENTICITY VERIFICATION
Evaluating evidence supporting the claimed origin or authenticity of the physical component.

RECORD INTEGRITY
Evaluating whether the associated digital information has been altered or remains consistent with its integrity controls.

These are separate functions and must not be treated as equivalent.

10. VERIFICATION AND EVIDENCE ARCHITECTURE

Verification status is associated with a defined verification method and supporting evidence.

A verification record identifies what was evaluated, the method used, the source of the evidence, the scope of the verification, and the relevant time.

Verification information may include:

- Entity identity
- Verification status
- Verification method
- Verification source
- Evidence reference
- Verification timestamp
- Verification scope
- Responsible organization or authority
- Result or outcome

The architecture distinguishes an asserted identity from an evidence-supported identity.

A registered record is not automatically an authenticated physical object.

A displayed verification label is not, by itself, proof of cryptographic verification.

Blockchain registration, cryptographic signing, manufacturer attestation, physical inspection, and evidence-backed identity validation are distinct mechanisms.

Their respective status and evidentiary scope must remain explicit.

11. INFORMATION COST AND SHARED IDENTITY

PITN connects information generated across the lifecycle of a physical product through persistent identity and structured relationships.

In robotics, information associated with one component is relevant to multiple organizational functions.

ENGINEERING

- Product identity
- Specifications
- Product revision
- Compatibility
- Configuration requirements
- Approved alternatives

PROCUREMENT

- Manufacturer
- Manufacturer part number
- Supplier
- Purchasing references
- Product documentation

MANUFACTURING

- Component identity
- Receiving records
- Product configuration
- Installation
- Manufacturing evidence

MAINTENANCE

- Physical component identity
- Manufacturer serial
- Installation history
- Service history
- Replacement information

QUALITY

- Inspection records
- Test results
- Verification status
- Evidence references
- Traceability

The shared identity architecture connects these information relationships.

Rather than reconstructing component information independently in each department, participating systems can reference the same persistent identity and associated records.

The resulting architecture reduces the need for repeated identification, manual reconciliation, duplicate data entry, and separate reconstruction of component history.

The economic value is associated with reduced information retrieval and reconciliation costs across the product lifecycle.

Any quantified savings claim requires measured operational evidence from an actual manufacturer or deployment. A specific financial saving is not implied by the architecture alone.

14. ARCHITECTURAL STATUS AND TERMINOLOGY

The PITN framework distinguishes documented architecture from operational software capability.

The following terminology applies:

EXISTING ARCHITECTURE

A data entity, identity relationship, namespace, or architectural concept documented in the framework.

DOCUMENTED PROTOTYPE

A proposed or described software structure, schema, API, or code concept that is not thereby established as tested software.

IMPLEMENTED

A software function or data structure demonstrated to exist in the relevant application or deployed environment.

TESTED

A function or system whose operation has been evaluated through identifiable tests and documented results.

VERIFIED

A defined identity claim or system property supported by specified evidence and a stated verification method.

These terms are not interchangeable.

The existence of an architectural specification does not establish that a corresponding production service has been deployed.

The existence of a demonstration does not establish that its displayed identity is backed by a live verification service.

15. ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE

PITN establishes a shared identity framework connecting physical assets and their associated information throughout their lifecycle.

Within robotics, ROBOPARTS.ai provides the component, product, installation, BOM, compatibility, and lifecycle information relationships.

Robot identities, component product identities, and physical component identities remain distinct while being connected through persistent references.

Digital passports present these relationships through a common identity and information structure.

The architecture is founded on the following principles:

ONE PERSISTENT IDENTITY FRAMEWORK.

SEPARATE IDENTITIES FOR ROBOTS, PRODUCTS, AND PHYSICAL COMPONENTS.

MANUFACTURER IDENTIFIERS ASSOCIATED WITH, NOT SUBSTITUTED FOR, PERSISTENT COMPONENT IDENTITIES.

SHARED DIGITAL PASSPORT PRESENTATION.

CONNECTED ENGINEERING CONFIGURATION AND PHYSICAL INSTALLATION BOM RELATIONSHIPS.

PERSISTENT LIFECYCLE AND TRACEABILITY RECORDS.

EVIDENCE-BASED VERIFICATION WITH EXPLICIT SCOPE.

STRUCTURED INFORMATION SHARED ACROSS THE PRODUCT LIFECYCLE.

16. CONCLUSION

The PITN identity architecture and ROBOPARTS.ai component information model establish a connected framework for identifying robots, products, and individual physical components.

The HUMANOIDO actuator identity example demonstrates the distinction between robot identity, actuator product identity, persistent component identity, and manufacturer serial number.

The Factory01 passport demonstrations illustrate the presentation of robot identity and associated status information.

The ROBOPARTS.ai data model provides the component, product, robot, BOM, installation, lifecycle, compatibility, and evidence relationships necessary to connect these identity concepts.

Together, these elements establish the architectural foundation for a unified robot and component passport structure.

The manufacturer example demonstrates how one persistent component identity connects engineering, procurement, manufacturing, maintenance, and quality information without treating each department's records as unrelated identities.

The resulting framework connects physical identity with structured digital information throughout the lifecycle of a robot and its components.

PITN provides the overarching identity architecture.

ROBOPARTS.ai provides the robotics component and lifecycle information model.

Digital passports present the connected identity and information relationships.
